# What keeps a Roblox game alive for years

Asked: which Roblox games last, how often they update, which social features Roblox ties to retention, what killed strong starts, and what makers of systemic sandbox games name as the cause of long life.
Made by one reader on Sonnet, 2026-11-21, from Roblox's documentation (read 2026-11-21), trade-press interviews, one trade-press analysis of a live chart, and developer-forum and guide-site coverage. First-hand developer postmortems were not found.

## What the sources say

### 1. Games that kept large counts for five years or more
- On a 99-game "Top Playing Now" chart, 11 games were made 2014-2019 and held 779,911 of 4,033,815 players (19%). Adopt Me! (2017) was third with 286,008; Murder Mystery 2 (2014) sixth with 111,296; Welcome to Bloxburg (2014) had 13,889. 42 games made in 2026 held 37%. Source: https://www.ggrecon.com/articles/roblox-top-games-didnt-exist-before-october/ (19 Nov 2026). This is one snapshot, not a trend.
- Adopt Me!: a director of business operations said "A lot of Roblox games will go up and down" and that the studio aims to be sustainable. Another quote from him ties the game to "feel fair" and to an in-house customer-support team. Neither is a stated cause of longevity. Sources: https://www.pocketgamer.biz/interview/76516/roblox-adopt-me-devs-launch-uplift-games/ (11 May 2021); summary of a later interview via a search result only (not opened).
- Brookhaven: Voldex's Chief Product Officer said returning players spend "20 minutes just chatting" and that social roleplay games like Brookhaven succeed without a traditional core loop. Source: https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/roblox-devs-say-socialization-is-what-makes-the-creator-led-sandbox-tick (1 May 2024).
- Jailbreak (April 2017): the Balfanz interview that would explain its life is behind a paywall; only the introduction was read. Source: https://gamesbeat.com/why-the-creat-alex-badcc-balfanz-interview/ (8 Sep 2025). A fan wiki (not opened) is reported by search to say monthly updates stopped on 29 March 2024.
- The developers' own account of why these games lasted: not found for Work at a Pizza Place, Brookhaven, Jailbreak or Adopt Me. See Not found.

### 2. Update frequency and the kinds of update that bring players back
- Roblox: content cadence "weekly to monthly" releases, depending on the developer; major updates may need "many months." Source: https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/production/game-design/liveops-essentials.md (page dated 2026-11-20).
- Roblox: many games release cadence updates "every two weeks to one month," and each should take "fewer than three weeks' effort." Source: https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/production/game-design/content-updates.md (2026-11-20).
- Roblox says without LiveOps updates even dedicated players lose interest once they finish the content. Limited-time events, seasonal events, collections and achievements, and season passes are named as what brings players back. Sources: liveops-essentials.md and content-updates.md, same dates.
- Roblox: a predictable schedule helps players return. Cadence content is items, furniture, pets, vehicles, weapons, maps, quests. A cadence update that adds new systems is an expansion and "not sustainable" as cadence. Source: content-updates.md.
- Roblox: limited-time events should take most players weeks to finish; permanent content added at the end of progression serves experienced players. Source: content-updates.md.
- Roblox: advance announcement of events lets players plan their return; track retention the week before and after an event. Source: https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/production/game-design/liveops-planning.md (2026-11-20).
- A Roblox staff member said many creators take early roadmap ideas from the community (for example Discord polls) and that cooperative LiveOps is growing. Source: https://developer.microsoft.com/en-us/games/articles/2023/02/the-art-of-liveops-jay-jodway-collaborative-creators/ (3 Feb 2023; a recap, not a transcript).

### 3. Social features Roblox ties to retention
- Roblox: social interaction supports retention; players become "evergreen sources of content for each other." Invitations can be built into play through parties and trading. Source: https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/production/game-design/design-for-roblox.md (2026-11-20).
- Roblox: guilds, groups, trading and parties "promote player interaction and collaboration"; players add variety and form friendships, "which encourage repeat visits." Source: liveops-essentials.md.
- Roblox: a strong social component "can massively accelerate growth and engagement." Friends can join each other's games; groups support socialising. Source: https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/production/roblox-user-base.md (2026-11-20).
- Roblox: private servers that are "prohibitively expensive" can discourage friends from playing together. "Locals" form almost all of a game's engaged base. Source: design-for-roblox.md.
- Voice: "Games with voice often see an uplift in engagement, DAU, and spending." No figure or date is given. Source: https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/chat/voice-chat.md (2026-11-20).
- Voldex (reported by Game Developer, 1 May 2024): "a third of our returning players are just socializing." In Driving Empire 66% of returning daily players completed a core action; in Dungeon Quest, 55%. These are the company's own figures, as reported. Source: the gamedeveloper.com link above.
- Visiting each other's builds: no Roblox guidance found.

### 4. What killed strong starts
- Garden Horizons (launched 22 Feb 2026): over 1 million concurrent after its first major update, about 23,000 within two weeks. Named causes: an under-tested update let exploiters duplicate high-value seeds; developers left and said the team was paid "pennies"; frame-rate drops; console support late; some players disliked paid Game Pass bonuses. Source: https://allthings.how/garden-horizons-player-count-drop-what-happened-and-is-the-game-dead/ (updated 18 Mar 2026). This is a guide site; the developer quotes are relayed from Discord, and the causes are reported, not confirmed.
- Fisch (Jan 2025): a money glitch, then a dupe exploit, then a data wipe and bans. Source: https://roonby.com/2025/01/19/roblox-fisch-data-wipe-infinite-money-hack-ban-wave/ (seen in search results only, not opened).
- Roblox: an unplanned event that left a surplus of in-game gold meant players had little reason to buy more; plan a sink for each new currency source. Source: https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/production/game-design/balance-virtual-economies.md (2026-11-20). The page does not mention duplication, exploits or pay-to-win.
- Roblox's monetization page does not say monetization harms retention. It says season passes are good for retention "when implemented well." Source: https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/production/game-design/monetization-foundations.md (2026-11-20).
- Developer posts say discovery and algorithm changes cut their player base. Source: https://devforum.roblox.com/t/roblox’s-algorithm-changes-are-killing-our-game/2417236 (search result only, not opened).
- Content running out: only Roblox's guidance names it (item 2). No developer postmortem found. Single-loop failure: not found.

### 5. Makers of systemic sandbox games outside Roblox
- Factorio (Wube, Friday Facts #365, 5 Feb 2021): 1.1 would be the last vanilla release; they rejected free updates forever because Factorio is "a little bit more of a niche game with a smaller but more dedicated audience"; one expansion "will not take less than a year." They kept mod support working. Source: https://www.factorio.com/blog/post/fff-365. FFF #438 (Space Age wrap-up) returned HTTP 403 and was not read.
- Terraria (Re-Logic): Redigit said "After 12 years the game still sells like hot cakes" and it is hard to move on; early sales paid for free updates; mods are called free advertising. Source: search summary of https://www.nme.com/news/terraria-developer-admits-its-hard-to-move-on-due-to-12-years-of-strong-sales-3464635 (not opened). The PC Gamer article (3 Jul 2023) could be read only to its subtitle.
- RimWorld (Tynan Sylvester, GDC 2017): the abstract says it was designed as a story generator, leaving features out led players to imagine what is not there, and development was deliberately unplanned. These are design claims, not claims about longevity. Source: https://gdcvault.com/play/1024686/-RimWorld-Contrarian-Ridiculous-and.
- Minecraft (Jens Bergensten): the goal is that starting to play ten or twenty years on "should feel similar, or close" to the start. Source: Eurogamer quote as relayed at https://onmsft.com/?p=180426 (second-hand; the Canberra Times 15-year interview returned HTTP 403).
- No source read has these makers naming a list of properties that give years of play. The statements above are about update models, mods and design intent.

## Where the sources disagree, or are weak
- Roblox says to update every two weeks to a month. A fan wiki, via a search summary, says Jailbreak stopped monthly updates in March 2024 and still has players. A mid-size count after that was reported as about 12,000 (a guide site, July 2026, search summary). Neither was opened. Cadence may matter less for some games, or may not; the sources do not settle it.
- Factorio's makers rejected free updates forever; Terraria's makers gave them for years. Their stated reasons differ (audience size, early sales).
- Roblox's retention claims (social, voice, events) come with no figures. Voldex's figures are one company's own and suggest a third of returning players socialise.
- The chart analysis is one day's snapshot. Older games' share (19%) says nothing about how long each stayed large.
- Most failure accounts are guide sites or forum posts. Garden Horizons is a short-life case, not a five-year one.
- Roblox's docs are the platform's advice, written to be applied to many games.

## What follows for someone building such a game
- Plan a small update every two to four weeks, and an expansion apart from it (2: content-updates.md).
- Make most cadence content out of existing systems, so it takes under three weeks (2).
- Add permanent content at the end of progression, and make each limited-time event last weeks (2).
- Give players reasons to meet each other: parties, trading, groups; keep private servers cheap (3: design-for-roblox.md).
- Expect some returning players to log in only to socialise, so a social place without a task counts (3: Voldex figures).
- Treat duplication as the failure to test for before an update ships (4: Garden Horizons, Fisch; both weak sources).
- Plan a sink for every new source of currency or item (4: balance-virtual-economies.md).
- Keep mod-like extension in mind: Factorio and Terraria credit it (5; second-hand for Terraria).

## Checked by a second reader
Checked 2026-11-21. 28 claims checked: 5 wrong or reworded, 5 unconfirmed, the rest confirmed. Each of the 8 "What follows" lines was also checked against its finding. Roblox pages were read as .md and show "last updated 2026-11-20".

### Wrong or reworded
- Item 4, Fisch, listed under "What killed strong starts". Brief: money glitch, dupe exploit, data wipe and bans. Source: the events are real (13-15 Jan 2025), but the source does not say they ended the game. The wipe applied only to players who used the glitch (for example daily streaks over 175 days), not to everyone. Cleanup began the same evening. Nothing opened shows a player drop. Address: https://roonby.com/2025/01/19/roblox-fisch-data-wipe-infinite-money-hack-ban-wave/ (read this time; the brief had it as search-only).
- Item 4, monetization. Brief quotes season passes as good for retention "when implemented well". Source has no such phrase. It says "With the right implementation, season passes can be great for day-to-day engagement and longer term retention." The meaning is the same; the quote marks are wrong. Address: https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/production/game-design/monetization-foundations.md
- Item 2, "not sustainable". Brief puts "not sustainable" in quotes for a cadence update that adds systems. Source: adding new systems "would be unsustainable for the creation of a content cadence", and such content "turns the content into an expansion". Address: https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/production/game-design/content-updates.md
- Item 1, Brookhaven. Brief: succeed without a "traditional core loop". Source: Rose says it lacks a "core loop"; the word "traditional" is not there. Speaker (Andrew Rose, chief product officer) and date (1 May 2024) are right. Address: https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/roblox-devs-say-socialization-is-what-makes-the-creator-led-sandbox-tick
- Item 4, Roblox economy page. Brief says "an unplanned event that left a surplus of in-game gold". Source: an underestimate of increased fishing in an event led to a surplus of Gold. The advice to plan sinks is there ("imperative to have plans for how to sink ... excess currency"). "Unplanned" is not the source's word. Address: https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/production/game-design/balance-virtual-economies.md

### Lines of "What follows" that go further than the evidence
- "Plan a small update every two to four weeks". Source: "many games" release every two weeks to one month; elsewhere "weekly to monthly". It describes what games do. It gives no reason this cadence works and no retention figure.
- "keep private servers cheap" (line 4). Source: do not make them "prohibitively expensive". "Cheap" is stronger. The groups part comes from liveops-essentials.md, not design-for-roblox.md, which the line names.
- "Expect some returning players to log in only to socialise, so a social place without a task counts" (line 5). The source gives one company's share (a third of returning players) for its own games. "A social place without a task counts" is in no source. It is a design conclusion.
- "Treat duplication as the failure to test for" (line 6). Rests on one guide-site account of Garden Horizons (relayed Discord quotes) and on Fisch. For Fisch, the source shows a fix within days and no collapse. The line's own "weak sources" tag is fair, but the line reads as advice.
- "Plan a sink for every new source of currency or item" (line 7). The source covers currency only (Gold, an event currency). "Or item" is not in it.
- "Keep mod-like extension in mind: Factorio and Terraria credit it" (line 8). Factorio says it keeps mod support working. The text read does not name mods as a cause of long life. The Terraria claim is unconfirmed (403). Item 5's own last line says no maker names such a list, so "credit it" goes further than that.
- Lines 1 to 3 match Roblox's own advice. They are platform advice. No source read shows that they cause retention.
