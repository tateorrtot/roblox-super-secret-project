# Roblox facts that constrain the design

Details and sources are in `docs/research/`, whose README says which file answers what. Roblox changed these roughly monthly through 2026; check the live docs page before relying on a number.
- **New games are shown first only to age-checked 16+ users.** Under-16s unlock after 250 plays by highly engaged 16+ players within 60 days (a drop to 100 was announced), or a refundable 50,000 Robux expedited review.
- **Home ranking** weighs play-through from the thumbnail, leaving within 60 and 180 seconds (negative), play days across days 1, 2-7 and 8-28, playtime up to 60 minutes a day, deliberately playing with friends, and days on which a player spends. Players acquired through ads never count.
- **Only near-identical copies are suppressed** as non-unique.
- **Servers stop when empty,** hold at most 200 players, and long-lived building games run 6 to 12. Progress saves to the player. A few shared numbers across servers are feasible; a shared live world is not.
- **Most play is on phones.** Do not rely on text chat, which is age-gated.
- **The higher 18+ payout rate** needs R15 avatars, or custom characters with enough joints, or no visible avatar. R6 disqualifies.
- **No shipped Roblox game keeps every good as a live physics object.** The ones that lasted make goods physical only while handled. The only budget found is roughly 40 to 60 moving assemblies in one player's view, from a community article.
- **Paid random items** need odds shown and are switched off in some regions.
- **Roblox's chat window lies over the top left of the screen and takes clicks there even when it has faded from sight** (seen 2026-11-19: 308 by 181 pixels in a small window). A screen of ours that may overlap it turns the chat off while it is open.
- **The strip along the top of the screen is 58 pixels high.** `UserInputService:GetMouseLocation` counts it, and so does a ScreenGui with `IgnoreGuiInset` on; `Camera:WorldToScreenPoint` and a GUI object's `AbsolutePosition` do not.
- **No property says that the camera is in first person.** Roblox's own camera scripts go into first person when the zoom falls below 1 stud, and then hold the camera 0.5 studs from `Camera.Focus`; otherwise the camera is its zoom, 1 stud or more, from `Camera.Focus`. They keep the way the camera looks from one frame to the next, so a script that sets `Camera.CFrame` once turns the view. They stop the view 80 degrees above and below level. Their module returns an empty table, so there is nothing to ask. Read on 2026-11-20 from the scripts in the Studio install: `ExtraContent/scripts/PlayerScripts/StarterPlayerScripts/PlayerModule.module/CameraModule/BaseCamera.lua` and `ClassicCamera.lua`.
- **Luau's new type solver can lose a narrowing.** A function typed `(...)?` that was narrowed with `~= nil` was still refused as "a value of type nil" when the table it came from had been returned by an `if` expression that narrowed something else: `return if selected ~= nil then find(selected.itemId) else nil`. Written as an `if` statement with a `return`, the same code passed (luau-lsp 1.70.1, 2026-11-20).
- **What Roblox has for input, and what its input actions were measured to do,** is in `input.md`.
- **`require("@game/ReplicatedStorage/...")` works in Studio** (checked 2026-11-19). The code does not use it: Roblox-side requires go through `game:GetService`, which is what luau-lsp follows with a Rojo sourcemap.
- **Parts that are welded together are one rigid body, which Roblox calls an assembly.** An assembly is one part or more, joined by `WeldConstraint`s or by joints that move. Its parts move as one. If one part is anchored, the whole assembly is anchored with it, and its mass counts as infinite. Anchoring every part of an assembly costs more than anchoring one, because each anchored part becomes an assembly of its own. The page does not say what happens when a weld is removed, nor how many parts an assembly may have. Read on 2026-11-21: https://create.roblox.com/docs/physics/assemblies
- **Destroying a weld splits an assembly at once, and the part left with no anchored part falls as one body.** Measured in Studio on 2026-11-21, on the server of a play test (`build/measure/welds.luau`). Five parts welded in a chain to an anchored part hung in mid-air, with the anchored part as their `AssemblyRootPart`. With the third weld destroyed, the first two parts stayed. The last three took a root part of their own and fell together, 16 studs in 0.4 seconds, the same distance apart as before. A chain of 300 parts took 0.018 seconds to make and weld, and while it hung `Stats.MovingPrimitivesCount` did not rise. Destroying its first weld took 0.0013 seconds, all 300 parts fell as one assembly, and the server's frame stayed at 16.6 milliseconds. That is one falling body in an empty place. It does not measure many trees, or a body that breaks up when it lands.
- **A script can read an image's pixels and build a mesh while the game runs,** with `EditableImage` and `EditableMesh`. It can load an image or a mesh that already exists only if the asset belongs to, or is shared with, whoever owns the game. A published game needs "Allow Mesh / Image APIs" switched on, under Security in its settings. Each client has a memory budget for them. An image or a mesh that starts blank needs no asset, so nobody has to own anything. Read on 2026-11-21 from the two classes' pages. **Studio needs the setting too.** Measured on 2026-11-21, on the client of a play test (`build/measure/images.luau`): `CreateEditableImage` and `CreateEditableMesh` each returned an object, and the first call that wrote a pixel or added a vertex failed with "EditableImage is not accessible. Go to the Security Tab in Experience Settings to enable this API." The setting is the user's to switch on.
- **A `ViewportFrame` shows parts inside a GUI,** through a `Camera` that is its `CurrentCamera`. It draws no shadows and no post-processing, and draws Neon and Glass at their lowest quality. The page says nothing of its cost. Read on 2026-11-21.
- **Luau's `vector` and Roblox's `Vector3` are one value while the game runs.** Measured in Studio on 2026-11-21, in edit mode (`build/measure/vector.luau`): `typeof(vector.create(1, 2, 3))` is `"Vector3"`; it equals `Vector3.new(1, 2, 3)`; a `vector` can be assigned to `Part.Position`; `vector.magnitude(part.Position)` works; either finds the other as a table's key; and `.x` and `.X` both read. So a library written with `vector`, which `lune` runs, takes a part's position as it is. Not checked: whether Studio's type checker accepts a `Vector3` where a `vector` is asked for.
- **`require` by string takes `./`, `../`, `@self/`, and `@game/` in Roblox, and no alias of our own yet.** `lune` and `luau-lsp` read aliases from a `.luaurc` file. So a require that must work in all three is a relative path, and the instances that Rojo builds must sit where the files do (`docs/research/23-roblox-first-local-libraries.md`). Read on 2026-11-21, and not run in Studio.
- **A body of welded parts drives on wheels that hang on hinges, wherever the wheels are.** Measured in Studio on 2026-11-21 (`gyms/vehicles`): a `HingeConstraint` as a motor spins each driving wheel, and a second one as a servo, about the vertical, steers a wheel. A body of 23 parts reached its top speed of 30 studs a second within 0.1, held a straight line to 0.23 studs in 97, and turned 59 degrees in 3 seconds. **A wheel must not collide with its own body:** a steering wheel that touched the deck's edge stopped the body turning, until a `NoCollisionConstraint` joined each wheel to each part of the body. A part whose one weld was destroyed while the body moved became its own assembly at once. Not tried: a player's client as the owner, springs, a slope, a phone.
